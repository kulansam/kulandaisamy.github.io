About Me

I am a postdoctoral research fellow at Boston Children’s Hospital / Harvard Medical School, mentored by Prof. Kaifu Chen with co-mentorship from Dr. Hong Chen. My research integrates vascular biology with computational genomics, focusing on endothelial cell identity regulation and inflammatory signaling in vascular disease.

I develop SCIG & CellFun, an AI-driven framework for identifying cell-identity genes (CIGs) by integrating gene expression with genetic sequence signatures, enabling improved network, trajectory, and pathway analyses in single-cell data.

Contribution to Science

1) Early Training in Structural Biology and Disease Mechanisms

In the early stages of my career at the Indian Institute of Technology Madras, I performed systematic analyses of protein–protein and nucleic acid complexes to identify key amino acid residues involved in protein stability and binding. I found that mutations in these residues were frequently linked to vascular diseases and neurological disorders. I also analyzed how disease-causing mutations alter protein structure, stability, and function, which motivated my commitment to understanding disease mechanisms.

2) Ph.D. Work: Large-scale Analyses of Disease-causing Mutations

As a Ph.D. graduate student at IIT Madras, I conducted large-scale analyses to elucidate molecular mechanisms of disease-causing mutations in vascular and neurological disorders using genomics and proteomics data. I observed that mutations at arginine residues frequently change to cysteine or glutamine and are associated with vascular disease. I also performed cross-correlation analyses across disease classes and found the strongest correlation between vascular and metabolic disorders. These efforts resulted in multiple first-author publications and conference presentations.

3) Postdoctoral Research: Endothelial Cell Identity and Atherosclerosis

As a postdoctoral research fellow at Boston Children’s Hospital / Harvard Medical School, I investigate endothelial cell identity regulation in atherosclerosis and other vascular diseases using transcriptomics, single-cell analysis, and mechanistic studies. I identified molecular mechanisms by which macrophage-specific epsins regulate lipid transport and inflammation, and showed that targeting epsins reduces foam cell formation and improves endothelial function. In parallel, I developed SCIG, a machine-learning framework that uncovers cell identity genes by integrating gene expression with genetic sequence codes.
